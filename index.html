SkEye-1

Phase 1 hover rig: build starts Nov 2026
Autonomous VTOL tiltrotor mapping UAV · designer and builder · 2026 – present

A twin wingtip tiltrotor that takes off like a helicopter, tilts its rotors forward to fly like a plane, and maps large areas with a camera. The rotors use swashplate cyclic pitch, like a helicopter's, to control the aircraft in hover.

What I did

  • Chose the configuration from simulation trade studies: tilt-only control couldn't hold a hover, so each rotor got a swashplate for cyclic pitch.
  • Developed custom PX4 flight-control modules in C++: a per-servo CCPM mixer with rate feed-forward, an rpm governor using bidirectional DShot telemetry, and a hover-to-cruise transition manager, each checked against a Python reference model.
  • Built an automated failsafe test matrix in SITL covering RC and datalink loss, low battery, geofence, GPS loss, mode switches and module failure at hover, mid-transition and cruise.
  • Modeled the rotor pods in parametric Python CAD with automated clash checks across the full tilt range, and sized the wing and tail (tail volume 0.40, twin-fin H-tail).

Results so far (simulation)

3 of 3full autonomous missions (~3 km: takeoff, transition, survey, landing) in PX4 + Gazebo
0.43 mworst altitude error across those missions
6 of 6module-failure recovery cases pass after fixing a control hand-over integrator wind-up
45 mskill switch to motor cut in the ground test; rotors below 100 rpm in 0.3 s

How it holds a hover

A swashplate tilts under each rotor, so every blade's pitch rises and falls once per turn. That tilts the rotor's thrust without changing its speed, which is far faster than speeding up or slowing down a 0.74 m rotor.

Both rotors tilting forward pitches the nose. One forward and one back yaws it. Different collective on each side rolls it.

Rocket motor thrust stand

Test equipment and data acquisition · designer and builder · 2026

A static stand that measures a rocket motor's thrust over time, so a motor's real performance can be checked against its rating.

What I did

  • Designed and built the stand around a 20 kg load cell, an HX711 amplifier and an ESP32 logging at 80 Hz (C++, Arduino framework).
  • Calibrated the load cell, wrote a repeatable test procedure, and fired the Estes C6, AeroTech G80-7T and AeroTech G125-14A.
  • Integrated the thrust curves in Python and MATLAB to get total impulse, and checked the result by hand.

Next version

  • A second stand with three load cells under a floating plate, to measure the side forces of a gimbaled motor for Icarus. CAD complete.

Icarus

Parts on order
Thrust-vector-controlled landing rocket · designer · 2026 – present

A finless, two-stage rocket that steers by gimbaling its motors and lands itself propulsively, with no parachute. Target apogee 450–500 m.

Design

  • The lower stage carries the boost motor on its own gimbal and drops at burnout. The upper stage lands on a second gimbaled motor with fold-out legs.
  • An 88 g CO₂ cold-gas system controls roll and attitude for the whole flight. The flight computer aborts at 15° of attitude error.
  • Coordinated a safety-code review with NAR, a regional launch club and AeroTech for staging, air-start and powered landing. Now looking for a Tripoli launch site, since the club's code requires a recovery system.

Test program

  1. Gimbal tests on the floating-plate stand

    Measure gimbal response and side force before anything flies.

  2. Single-motor TVC ascent

    Long-burn AeroTech I59 motor, so the controller has time to prove itself.

  3. Two-stage landing flights

    38 mm AeroTech H100W and H125W motors.

Next: a methalox engine

Design phase

A 1,000 lbf liquid-oxygen and methane rocket engine for static-fire testing, the same propellants as SpaceX's Raptor and Blue Origin's BE-4. Pressure-fed, with a heat-sink copper chamber for short first burns. Design and safety review first, then cold-flow tests, then hot fires.
